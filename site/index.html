Why Agentman?

Agentman is an open-source, account-free home for your coding agents, with live status, one-tap answers, plan reviews and file and diff views, over a relay that stores nothing.

WorkingIdleNeeds you

Every dot is an agent running on your Mac.

An animation of agents on a Mac: most are working or idle, one stops to ask a question and turns orange, and an answer from the phone sends it back to work.

See it live

Every session on your Mac, grouped by what needs you first — and every one you have ever run, by folder. Open one to follow along, or to pick it back up.

Answer in one tap

Permission prompts and questions arrive as a notification and a card. Tap an answer, or say no and tell the agent what to do instead.

Send the next step

Message a running agent from your phone. It's typed into the terminal, or handed over when the turn ends.

Beyond the transcript

See what your agent changed.

The conversation tells you what happened. The workspace lets you look for yourself.

Files

Browse the workspace.

Open folders in the agent's working directory and find the file you want. Code and images open in a read-only viewer; private files and symlinks stay hidden.

Changes

Review the actual diff.

See staged, unstaged and new files since the last commit. Open a change to compare lines, with additions and removals clearly marked.

Read code and view images

Open source files with syntax highlighting and line numbers, or inspect an image right on your phone.

Open the live site

Open a server your agent started in the app. Share a preview link or stop the server from your phone.

Send images with your message

Attach a screenshot to show what you mean. Images your agent produces or inspects also open from the conversation.

From anywhere

Stay in charge of the work.

Every agent, not just one: the same controls for Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Kiro and Antigravity.

Review the plan first

Plans and walkthroughs an agent writes open on your phone. Approve them, or send them back with a note.

Switch mode and model

Move an agent into plan mode or onto another model mid-session. If a switch would change your Mac's defaults, it asks first.

Say no, and say why

Refuse an action and the agent hears what to do instead, without leaving the question it asked.

The moment it's built for

When your agent stops to ask, your phone asks you.

Permission prompts and multiple-choice questions show up on your phone the moment they appear in the terminal.

  1. The agent blocks. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Kiro or Antigravity shows a prompt in its terminal, or OpenCode raises one through its API.
  2. Your phone buzzes. The question appears as a card, with the full command in view.
  3. You tap once. Your choice goes back to the agent and it carries on — with your note, if you said no.

Get started

Install the command-line tool, pair your phone, start an agent.
$curl -fsSL https://agentman-nu.vercel.app/install | sh

Or with Homebrew: brew install lenajeremy/agentman/agentman. Either way you get tmux too, which Agentman uses to type into your agents.

$curl -fsSL https://agentman-nu.vercel.app/install | sh

For amd64 and arm64. Homebrew on Linux works too: brew install lenajeremy/agentman/agentman. Or build from a clone with Go 1.26.

Open the TestFlight invite

The app is in public beta on TestFlight. Open the invite on your iPhone and you'll get each new build as it ships.

Build the app from source with Expo. It runs and shows alerts while it's open; background push isn't set up on Android yet. Mobile setup guide

Then, on your computer
  1. am install-hooksconnect your agents, once
  2. am servestart Agentman, leave it running
  3. am pairscan the code with the app
  4. am claudestart an agent you can answer
